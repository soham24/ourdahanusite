# Getting to Dahanu from Mumbai

Dahanu sits about 130 km north of Mumbai, near the Gujarat border, and it is genuinely easy to reach — most people come up on a Western Railway local for the price of a cup of coffee. The confusion usually isn't *how* to get here. It's the small things nobody tells you: which station you actually want, what to pay the auto driver, and which turn to take off the highway.

Here's what you need.

## First: Dahanu Road, not Dahanu

If you're searching train times or booking a ticket, the station you want is **Dahanu Road (station code DRD)**.

"Dahanu" refers to the wider town and taluka. "Dahanu Road" is the railway station and the area around it. They aren't two different places to choose between — but if you type just "Dahanu" into a booking app and get confused results, this is why.

Everything below assumes you're heading for Dahanu Road.

## By train

The train is the sensible option. Dahanu Road is the northern terminus of the Western Railway suburban line, so you can get here on an ordinary local — no reservation, no planning, just turn up.

**Roughly how long it takes**

| From | Approximate journey |
|---|---|
| Borivali | 1 hr 45 min – 2 hrs by local |
| Borivali | 1 hr 20 – 40 min by faster/express service |
| South Mumbai (Churchgate side) | 2.5 – 3 hrs depending on service |

**Where to board**

- **Coming from South Mumbai?** Churchgate or Mumbai Central.
- **Coming from the suburbs?** Borivali is the easiest boarding point if you can get there conveniently, and it cuts a good chunk off the journey.
- **Already north?** Virar has Dahanu services too.

**On fares:** check the current price in the UTS app or at the counter rather than trusting any number you read online, including ours. Suburban fares are cheap — this is not the expensive part of your trip — but they change, and a stale figure helps nobody.

**Do you need to book?**

- **Local trains:** no. Walk up, buy a ticket, board.
- **Express or reserved class:** yes, book ahead.

**A few practical things**

- **Avoid peak-hour locals if you're carrying luggage.** The Virar-side commuter crush is real, and a weekend bag in a rush-hour local is a miserable experience for you and everyone around you. Travel mid-morning or early if you can.
- **Ladies' coach position varies** by rake and platform — check the platform indicators rather than assuming it'll be where it was last time.
- **Timetables change.** Western Railway publishes current Dahanu EMU schedules; check those for the first and last services of the day rather than relying on a fixed list.

## Arriving at Dahanu Road — and what the auto should cost

This is the part most guides skip, and it's the part that actually saves you money.

Autos are normally waiting outside Dahanu Road station. You don't need to pre-book anything for ordinary travel around town.

**What locals actually pay:**

| Trip | Fare |
|---|---|
| Shared auto to Parnaka | **₹20 per person** |
| Whole auto ("special") to Parnaka or Dahanu Fort | **₹100** |
| Shared, Ashagar / Mahalaxmi side | **₹30 – ₹50 per person** |

Two things worth understanding:

**Sharing is normal here.** A shared auto runs a fixed route and picks up several passengers — that's the ₹20-per-person option. A "special" means you've hired the whole vehicle for yourself, which is why it's ₹100 rather than ₹20.

**Agree the fare before you get in.** Dahanu autos don't work like Mumbai's metered ones, particularly for longer trips. This isn't anyone trying to cheat you — it's just how it works — but state where you're going and confirm the price first, and use the numbers above as your reference point.

**Heading to Bordi?** That's considerably further — Maharashtra Tourism puts it around 17 km from Dahanu — so expect a proper negotiated fare rather than a short local hop.

There are shops, tea and basic food around the station and in town, so you don't need to arrive stocked up.

## By road

**The route:** Mumbai → Western Express Highway / NH48 towards Gujarat → **Charoti** → Dahanu.

**Distance:** roughly 130–150 km depending on where in Mumbai you start.

**Driving time:** typically 3–4 hours, heavily traffic-dependent.

**The turn people get wrong:** you leave NH48 around **Charoti**, heading toward Dahanu and the Mahalaxmi side. Miss it and you'll be well on your way to Gujarat before you notice. Set it in your maps app before you start rather than looking for signage at speed.

**Stopping to eat:** the highway restaurants around **Manor** and **Charoti** are the usual break, and Charoti is a natural pause since you're turning off there anyway.

**Tolls and fuel:** we're deliberately not printing a figure. It depends on your vehicle and current fuel prices, and a wrong number is worse than none — work it out for your own car before you leave.

**Parking at the beaches** is generally available, but weekends and festival dates get busy. Arrive earlier rather than circling.

## When to travel

**Leave early.** Especially on weekends. An early-morning departure from Mumbai is the difference between a relaxed drive and a long one, and it gets you here with the day still ahead of you.

**Come back before Sunday evening.** The late-Sunday return into Mumbai is exactly as slow as you'd expect.

**Weekday vs weekend:** weekdays are much quieter. Beaches and resorts are noticeably busier at weekends — still nothing like Juhu, but if you're picturing an empty shoreline, come midweek.

### Monsoon: read this before you book

Heavy rain genuinely affects travel here, and it's worth planning around rather than hoping.

- **Rail disruption is a real possibility.** Flooding in the Palghar area caused cancellations and diversions as recently as July 2026. If you're travelling in heavy monsoon, check service status the morning you leave.
- **Roads are slower** and visibility can be poor on the highway stretch.
- **The sea is rough.** Monsoon is not the swimming season here — come for the green landscape and the quiet, not the water.

Monsoon Dahanu is beautiful, and plenty of people prefer it. Just don't arrive expecting a beach holiday.

## The short version

- Take a Western Railway local to **Dahanu Road (DRD)**. From Borivali it's about two hours.
- No booking needed for locals. Book ahead only for reserved/express.
- Avoid peak hours if you have luggage.
- Outside the station: **₹20 shared to Parnaka, ₹100 for a special.** Agree the fare first.
- Driving: NH48 north, **turn off at Charoti**. Three to four hours.
- Leave Mumbai early. Return before Sunday evening.
- In monsoon, check train status before you set out and don't plan on swimming.

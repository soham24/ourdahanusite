# Mahalaxmi, Udvada and Bahrot Caves

Three very different places, and worth understanding before you go — particularly Udvada, where there's an access restriction visitors should know about in advance rather than discover at the gate.

---

## Mahalaxmi Temple

The major temple of the area, at Vivalvedhe near Charoti — Palghar district administration puts it roughly **4 km from Charoti**, right off the Mumbai–Ahmedabad route.

**Getting there:** easy by road, and it's on the way in if you're driving up from Mumbai. Auto or taxi from Dahanu works too.

**Timings:** confirm with the temple before you go. We're not printing hours that might have changed.

**Dress:** modest clothing, standard Indian temple etiquette.

**How long:** 30–60 minutes for a normal visit, excluding queue time. Longer during festivals — considerably longer.

### The Mahalaxmi Yatra

The main festival is a **roughly 15-day yatra beginning around Hanuman Jayanti** (usually March or April, depending on the lunar calendar). It draws very large crowds, with substantial tribal participation and **Tarpa dancing** — one of the best times to see living Warli culture rather than a performance of it.

**Navratri** (September–October) is the other high-crowd period, with long queues possible.

If you're visiting during either, book accommodation earlier and expect the whole area to be busier.

---

## Udvada

Across the Gujarat border, roughly an hour by road from Dahanu or Bordi depending on where you start and the traffic. There's also a train option via Udvada station plus local transport.

Udvada is **one of the most sacred centres of Zoroastrianism in India**, home to the Iranshah Atash Behram.

### Please read this before you go

**Non-Parsis are not permitted inside the Iranshah Atash Behram.**

This isn't a queue or a ticket issue — it's a religious restriction, and it's absolute. Turning up expecting to go in and being refused is an unhappy experience for everyone, and the fault would be with the planning, not the community.

### What a general visitor can do

Plenty, and it's a worthwhile trip:

- Walk the historic town and its **heritage Parsi houses and streets** — architecturally distinctive and genuinely lovely
- View the sacred precinct respectfully from the permitted public areas
- Explore the town and the coast
- **Eat.** For a lot of visitors this is the main event

**Parsi food is the reason most non-Parsi visitors make the trip**, and it's a good reason. Plan to eat here rather than treating it as a quick stop.

Photography: observe posted restrictions and don't photograph people or religious spaces without asking.

## Bahrot Caves

The most demanding of the three, and the most rewarding if you're up for it.

**Where:** near the Bordi/Aswali side, roughly **8 km from Bordi** to the start of the trek.

**The trek:** about **4 km, and steep.** This is not a roadside attraction you park at. Allow roughly **half a day** overall — actual up-and-down time depends heavily on fitness.

**Who it suits:** reasonably fit walkers. **Not suitable** for very young children, elderly visitors, or anyone with knee or mobility problems. Be realistic about this rather than finding out halfway up.

**When:** cooler months. Some sources suggest August to February. **Avoid heavy monsoon days** — the steep trail gets slippery and it stops being fun quickly.

**Take:** plenty of water, shoes with grip, snacks, and sun or rain protection depending on the season. **A guide is advisable if it's your first time.**

**What's at the top:** caves and rock-cut cisterns, remains tied to the site's history as a **Zoroastrian refuge**, and hill views across the region. Bahrot matters a great deal to Parsi and Zoroastrian history — the community sheltered here with the sacred fire, which is what connects this hillside to Udvada down on the coast.

Doing Bahrot and Udvada on the same trip makes that history legible in a way neither does alone.

---

## Planning it

- **Mahalaxmi** slots neatly into your arrival or departure drive — it's near Charoti, where you turn off anyway.
- **Udvada** is a half-day, and should include lunch.
- **Bahrot** is a half-day minimum and needs an early start and decent weather.

Trying to do all three in one day would be a bad weekend. Pick two.
